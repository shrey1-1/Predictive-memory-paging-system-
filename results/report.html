Phase 12a · Robustness

The combined system holds on 22 real traces with no retuning

22 real traces × 2 memory sizes = 44 points, LRU underneath. The final system has fewer faults than readahead alone in 34 of 44 points and ties in 8. The weak case is bwaves-s2 (8-13 frames). milc and libquantum are pure streaming (LRU = OPT = working set), so their gain is readahead removing first-touch faults; lbm has no predictable pattern at all. The one large loss to readahead is sphinx3 at 1%, where the decider holds back useful streams. Gated = readahead confidence scaled by stream length so the decider can stop short streams. With ghost-list feedback it halves the worst case but costs part of the mcf gain; with shadow-directory feedback (Phase 14) it keeps the mcf gain and has the best worst case of all systems. Phase 14 · Feedback estimate

A shadow directory gives the decider the true fault delta

The ghost list only charges harm when the page a prefetch directly evicted comes back, so on the synthetic clustered@9 it reported "winning" while prefetching lost 21.5%. The shadow directory is a no-prefetch copy of the eviction policy that stores page IDs only (8 bytes per tracked frame, about 0.2% of RAM with 4 KB pages). With every page tracked it measures the true per-epoch fault delta exactly; hash-sampling 1/8 of the pages (SHARDS) gave the same fault counts on 9 of 10 real traces at 5% memory. On clustered@9 the combined system goes from −21.5% to −2.5% vs LRU. On the real traces the two estimates lead to different fault counts in 9 of 44 points (amber = directory better):

Phase 12b · Cost of extra I/O

The gain depends on how expensive one prefetch is

Fault counts hide the disk reads that prefetching adds. c* = (LRU faults − faults) / prefetches is the highest cost of one prefetch, as a fraction of one fault's stall, at which a system still beats LRU (0% = fully hidden I/O, 100% = as costly as a fault). Every value is below 100%, so no system wins at every cost. The original Markov system uses I/O most efficiently where it helps; the combined system saves more faults but needs cheaper prefetches. No disk latency is assumed and prediction CPU cost is not included. RAM = 1% of working set.

Phase 15 · Time model

Faster on a parallel SSD, slower on a single-channel HDD; in time, the final system ties readahead

An I/O timeline replays every run: compute time per access, a prediction cost per access, 4 KB reads of 80 µs on an NVMe SSD with 8 parallel channels or 5 ms on an HDD with 1 channel. Demand faults block and can queue behind prefetch reads; a prefetched page used before its read finishes stalls for the rest (a late prefetch). Speedup = time(LRU) ÷ time(system); amber = faster than LRU, red = slower. These are model estimates with stated latencies, not measurements; compute time per access is unknown for these traces, so it is swept.

The original Markov system cuts faults by 35–37% on leslie3d and bwaves but is not faster: it prefetches one access ahead, so its reads arrive late. Readahead fetches a window ahead and arrives in time. The final system has fewer faults than readahead in most configurations, but in estimated time the two are level on average: it wins on leslie3d, loses on sphinx3.

Phase 18 · Component comparison

Every predictor and every decision engine, one change at a time

Same programs (every real trace at 1% memory, plus the synthetic clustered trace as a danger test), same LRU underneath; only one component changes per row. Speed-ups use the Phase 15 time model (1 µs compute per request, 0.2 µs prediction cost). Amber = best mean in the table; red = worse than LRU.

Two readings. Accuracy does not decide the winner: readahead is exactly right about the next page only ~3% of the time but removes the most faults of any single predictor, because it fetches pages never seen before. The decision engine matters most in the danger case: "always" gets the full benefit on real programs but is badly hurt on clustered; fixed thresholds are safe but lose half the benefit; the final directory engine keeps the benefit and limits the harm. Fed the original Markov predictor, all engines give about the same result, because that predictor rarely has anything worth prefetching.

Design decisions

Six ideas kept, seven rejected or not adopted

Each idea was measured on the warm-up portion (first 25% of each trace) before any full-trace run. Negative results are kept because they explain where the gains come from.

  • Kept

    Readahead + Markov combination

    Readahead covers new pages in a run, Markov covers repeats; better than either alone in most configurations.

  • Kept

    Delta-Markov tier

    Learns page gaps, so it predicts unseen pages. Markov alone on bwaves: 1,969 → 810 faults.

  • Kept, partial

    Gating readahead by stream length

    Halves the worst case and fixes most of clustered@6; costs about half the mcf gain; no effect on clustered@9, where the ghost estimate says "winning" (+988) while the true delta is −1,250.

  • Rejected

    Evicting unused prefetches early

    Never better than LRU on the warm-up data; a longer grace period is much worse.

  • Rejected

    Protecting the predicted page from eviction

    No change: predicted pages are already resident and recent, so LRU keeps them anyway.

  • Rejected

    Longer minimum stream length

    Fixes clustered only by losing the graph and leslie3d gains.

  • Rejected

    Smoothed confidence

    Slightly worse on the warm-up data.

  • Rejected

    Different ghost refault window (250-2,000)

    No effect on faults.

  • Kept

    Shadow directory feedback (Phase 14)

    Exact fault delta from a page-ID-only copy of the policy; clustered@9 from -21.5% to -2.5% vs LRU; samplable to 1/8 with the same decisions on 9 of 10 traces.

  • Kept

    Faster controller (Phase 16)

    Epoch 1,000 and step 0.1, chosen on warm-up data only; halves the warm-up worst case; identical elsewhere.

  • Kept

    No prefetch budget cap (Phase 17)

    The 35% cap from Stage 5 blocked omnetpp while it was winning every epoch; removing it hurt no warm-up trace.

  • Not adopted

    Top-2 prefetching

    Big win on leslie3d, but worse on bfs-3 and bwaves.

  • Rejected

    Off switch with exponential backoff (Phase 14)

    Never triggered on any program.

Source: docs/RESULTS.md sections 9-12

Limitations

What these numbers do not show

  • Metric

    Mostly page-fault counts. The time estimates (Phase 15) use stated typical latencies and a swept compute time per access; they are a model, not measurements on real hardware.

  • Traces

    Last-level-cache traces at 4 KB page granularity approximate OS paging; they are not OS paging traces.

  • Runs

    One run per configuration (the traces are deterministic), 200,000 accesses each, small simulated memories (8 to 1,163 frames).

  • Baselines

    ARC drops the textbook tie-break; LRU-2 has no correlated-reference period; the readahead window is a quarter of RAM.

  • Tuning

    Controller and ghost-list parameters are still the untuned defaults; the graph traces' warm-up portion is a sequential initialisation and could not inform any choice.
